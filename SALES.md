ship.** Every merged PR re-films a narrated video walkthrough of what your
product now does, on a board a stakeholder can open with zero setup. Comments
on a walkthrough dispatch change requests back to your coding agent.

## The two-minute demo, in exact order

Rehearse against the seeded workspace: `/s/demo` (public, no login) and
`board-acme-landing` (logged in). Every step below exists in the product
today — nothing in this script is vapour.

**0:00 — Open the share link.** `/s/demo`, in a clean browser profile, no
login. Say: *"This is what your PM sees. They never cloned the repo, never
installed anything."* Let them notice it loads instantly.

**0:15 — Play the walkthrough.** Open the latest walkthrough frame, hit
play. Let ~15 seconds of narrated video run. Say nothing while it plays —
the product is talking.

**0:35 — Show "what changed".** Point at the What-changed list under the
video: two steps unchanged, one changed, each with a one-line reason. Then
the predecessor frame sitting beside it. Say: *"Last week's version is right
there. Nobody wrote a changelog."*

**0:50 — The honesty trick.** Show the take manifest (segments with
sha256). Say: *"The unchanged steps aren't 're-rendered and similar' —
they're byte-identical to last week's, provably. When Foldo says nothing
else changed, nothing else changed."* This is the moment technical founders
lean in.

**1:10 — Merge → new frame, live.** Trigger a render (merge a prepared PR
on the demo repo, or hit "Render now" in the Docs modal). The new frame
lands beside its predecessor showing *queued → capturing → rendering →
ready*, live on the board. While it films: *"That's a real browser filming
the real app. If a selector broke, you'd get a still with a caption and an
amber badge — not a dead pipeline."*

**1:35 — Close the loop.** Drop a comment pin on the new walkthrough:
*"The trial CTA should say how long the trial is."* Click **Make this an
edit** → Send. Show the dispatch streaming to done and the result frame.
Say: *"Your stakeholder just filed a change request straight to the coding
agent — from the documentation."*

**1:55 — The ask.** *"£79 a month per product, 14 days free, setup is a
GitHub App install and a URL. Which repo should we point it at?"*

## The three objections you WILL hear, with honest answers

### "Why not Loom?"

Loom is a person recording a video once. It's accurate the day it's
recorded and stale by the next merge — and nobody ever re-records. If your
team merges agent-written PRs weekly, a Loom library is a museum of old
UIs. Foldo's walkthroughs are regenerated by the pipeline on every merge;
the artifact structurally cannot rot.

**Honest part:** if you want a human face explaining a decision once —
onboarding a hire, a design rationale — Loom is better and we don't replace
it. Foldo replaces the *maintained* walkthrough, the one nobody maintains.

### "Why not Arcade?"

Arcade (and Storylane, Navattic) is demo *authoring*: a human hand-builds
one polished interactive tour, usually for the marketing site. Great at
that. But it's manual — when the product changes, someone re-captures and
re-edits. Foldo is documentation *infrastructure*: unattended, diff-driven,
per-merge, with provable segment reuse. Nobody "authors" a take; the merge
does.

**Honest part:** an Arcade demo is prettier than a Foldo take today. If you
need one hand-crafted flagship demo for the website, use Arcade. If you
need this week's product documented every week without a human in the loop,
that's us.

### "Why not just read the PR?"

You might! Engineers should. But the people this is for — the PM, the
designer, the founder reviewing what three coding agents shipped this week
— don't read diffs, and agent PRs come too fast and too verbose even for
the engineers. The PR says what the code does; the walkthrough shows what
the *product* does now, in the words a stakeholder uses.

**Honest part:** Foldo documents visible behaviour. A refactor, a
performance fix, a security patch won't show up in a walkthrough — the PR
is still the record for those, and we say so rather than pretend otherwise.

## What a design partner will actually test first

The riskiest assumption: **unattended grounded capture works on THEIR app**
— their auth wall, their feature flags, their flaky selectors — reliably
enough that the walkthrough is trusted without a human checking it each
merge. Everything else (pricing, board UX, dispatch loop) only matters if
the first take of their real product films clean or degrades gracefully
enough to still be useful. Design the pilot around proving exactly that in
the first 24 hours.
